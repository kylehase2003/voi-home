    Due Diligence Checklist Before You Wire a Deposit in Turkey

    The documents and checks you should see before any money leaves your account when buying property in Turkey.

    International bank transfers are effectively irreversible. A deposit sent before the right checks is a deposit you may not see again if something goes wrong. Most property purchases in Turkey go smoothly, but the problems that do occur are almost always ones a careful check would have caught: a seller who is not the only owner, a mortgage on the title, a building without an occupancy permit, or a price far above the property's value.

    Before you pay anything substantial for a property in Turkey, make sure you have seen and understood the following. This guide explains each check, why it matters and how to do it.

    The checklist

    1. The title deed (Tapu) record. Confirm that the seller is the registered owner and check the type of title. See our Tapu guide.
    2. Mortgages, liens and annotations. A current land registry extract shows any mortgage, attachment by creditors, or annotation that could affect your purchase.
    3. Permits. The building permit and, for completed buildings, the occupancy permit (iskan). Without iskan, utilities, resale and some legal processes become harder.
    4. The developer's record for new projects: completed projects, delivery history and company standing.
    5. An independent valuation by a licensed valuer, which foreign buyers need anyway, and which tells you whether the price is reasonable.
    6. The contract and payment plan in writing, with the exact unit, price, currency, payment dates, delivery date and what happens if either side does not perform.
    7. A clear breakdown of costs: title deed fees, VAT if any, valuation, translation, legal fees and any commission. See our costs guide.
    8. Debts linked to the property, such as unpaid property tax, aidat or utility bills.

    1. The title deed in detail

    The title deed shows who owns the property, the size and location of the unit, and its type. For apartments, there are two main types. Full condominium ownership (kat mülkiyeti) is issued once a building is complete and registered. Construction servitude (kat irtifakı) is used while a building is under construction or before the condominium title has been issued. Both can be bought, but full ownership gives more certainty. Check that every owner listed on the deed is part of the sale: if a property has several owners, all of them must sign.

    2. Mortgages, liens and annotations

    The land registry record shows any mortgage held by a bank, any attachment placed by a creditor, and any annotations such as rights of way, lease registrations or restrictions. A mortgage is not necessarily a problem, as long as it will be paid off and removed at or before the transfer, but this must be written into the contract and confirmed on the day.

    3. Building and occupancy permits

    The building permit (yapı ruhsatı) shows that the building was approved. The occupancy permit (iskan) confirms that it was completed in line with that approval and is fit to live in. Buildings without iskan can face problems with utility connections, mortgages, resale and, in some cases, citizenship applications. For older buildings, also ask whether any part of the building was added without permission, such as extra floors or enclosed balconies.

    4. The developer's record

    For off-plan or newly completed projects, research the developer as carefully as the property. Visit completed projects, ask residents about quality and delivery, and check whether earlier projects were delivered on time. A developer with a long record of completed buildings is a far safer counterparty than a new company with a single project.

    5. Independent valuation

    Foreign buyers in Turkey need a valuation report from a licensed valuer before the title deed transfer. Use it to your advantage. Ask for the valuation early, before you commit, so you know whether the price is in line with the market. For citizenship applications, the valuation must meet the investment threshold.

    6. The contract

    A good contract names the exact unit (block, floor, door number and title deed details), the price and currency, the payment schedule, the delivery date for off-plan property, the specifications and finishes, and what happens if either side does not perform. For off-plan purchases, ask for the contract to be signed before a notary, which gives it stronger legal standing.

    7. The full cost

    Title deed fees are usually 4% of the declared value, commonly shared between buyer and seller by agreement. Add the valuation report, sworn translation, notary fees, legal fees, any agency commission, compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK), utility connections and, for some new properties, VAT. Knowing the full figure in advance avoids surprises on transfer day.

    8. Debts attached to the property

    Ask the seller for proof that property tax, aidat and utility bills are paid up to date. Unpaid aidat can become a dispute with the building management, and unpaid utility bills can delay new connections.

    If you are buying for citizenship or residence

    Confirm before paying that the property qualifies, including its value, its history and, for residence permits, its location. Payments for citizenship must also follow the banking procedure required for the application. Properties previously owned by foreigners, bought from relatives or still under construction may raise additional questions.

    Other checks worth making

    • Military and security zones: foreign buyers need a clearance that the land registry requests as part of the process. Some areas are restricted.
    • Earthquake resilience: for older buildings, consider an engineering assessment.
    • Building management: ask about the building's rules, reserves and planned works.
    • Tenancy: if the property is let, check the lease, the rent and the tenant's rights.

    Who should do these checks?

    An independent lawyer working for you, not for the seller, is the best protection. A good agent will hand you this checklist without being asked and help collect the documents. Never feel pressured to pay a deposit before the checks are done: a genuine seller will understand.
